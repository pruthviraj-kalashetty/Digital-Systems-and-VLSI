# ◈ Comparator

[![Stage](https://img.shields.io/badge/Combinational--Circuits-blue.svg)](#)
[![Focus](https://img.shields.io/badge/Focus-Comparators-orange.svg)](#)

This module introduces digital comparators, fundamental combinational circuits used to compare two binary numbers and determine their relative magnitude. It covers 1-bit, 2-bit, and 3-bit comparators, including their truth tables, Boolean expressions, logic implementation, and comparison operations.

Comparators are widely used in arithmetic units, control logic, processors, digital systems, address decoding, and VLSI/ASIC designs.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By working through this module, you will be able to:

- Understand the working principle of digital comparators.
- Analyze the relationship between two binary input values.
- Design and analyze 1-bit, 2-bit, and 3-bit comparators.
- Understand comparator truth tables and Boolean expressions.
- Determine whether one binary number is greater than, less than, or equal to another.
- Build a foundation for comparison logic used in digital and VLSI systems.

## 🛠️ Core Concepts Covered

### 1. Comparator Fundamentals

Understand digital comparators as combinational circuits that compare two binary numbers and generate outputs indicating whether one input is greater than, less than, or equal to the other.

### 2. 1-Bit Comparator

Study the comparison of two single-bit inputs and understand the generation of:

- **A > B**
- **A < B**
- **A = B**

### 3. 2-Bit Comparator

Learn how two-bit binary numbers are compared by evaluating the most significant bits first and then considering the lower-order bits when required.

### 4. 3-Bit Comparator

Understand the comparison of three-bit binary numbers using a hierarchical comparison of their respective bit positions.

### 5. Comparator Logic

Understand how logic gates such as AND, OR, NOT, and XNOR can be combined to implement magnitude comparison functions.

### 6. Practical Applications

Comparators are used in:

- Arithmetic and control circuits
- Digital processors
- Address comparison
- Control systems
- Data selection circuits
- Sorting and decision logic
- VLSI and ASIC designs
