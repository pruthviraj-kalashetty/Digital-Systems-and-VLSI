# N-Type Semiconductor

## 📖 Overview

An **N-Type Semiconductor** is an **extrinsic semiconductor** formed by adding a **small amount of pentavalent (5-valence-electron) impurity** to a pure semiconductor such as **silicon (Si)** or **germanium (Ge)**.

The added impurity provides **extra free electrons**, making **electrons the majority charge carriers**. This increases the electrical conductivity of the semiconductor.

---

# Definition

> **An N-Type Semiconductor is an extrinsic semiconductor formed by doping a pure semiconductor with pentavalent impurity atoms, where electrons are the majority charge carriers.**

---

# Why is it Needed?

An N-Type semiconductor is needed because it:

- Increases electrical conductivity.
- Provides more free electrons for current flow.
- Forms PN junctions when combined with P-Type semiconductors.
- Is used to manufacture diodes, transistors, MOSFETs, and integrated circuits.
- Enables high-speed electronic devices.

Without N-Type semiconductors, many modern electronic devices would not function properly.

---

# Working Principle

An N-Type semiconductor is formed by adding **pentavalent impurities** to pure silicon or germanium.

Common pentavalent impurities are:

- Phosphorus (P)
- Arsenic (As)
- Antimony (Sb)

Each impurity atom has **5 valence electrons**.

- Four electrons form covalent bonds with neighboring silicon atoms.
- The **fifth electron** is weakly bound and becomes a **free electron**.
- These free electrons move easily through the crystal and carry electric current.

As a result:

- **Majority charge carriers:** Electrons
- **Minority charge carriers:** Holes

# Working Example

Suppose a pure silicon crystal is doped with **Phosphorus (P)**.

- Phosphorus has **5 valence electrons**.
- Four electrons form covalent bonds with neighboring silicon atoms.
- The fifth electron becomes free to move.

Result:

- More free electrons are available.
- Electrical conductivity increases.
- The semiconductor becomes an **N-Type semiconductor**.

---

# Applications

N-Type semiconductors are used in:

- PN Junction Diodes
- Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJTs)
- MOSFETs
- CMOS Circuits
- Integrated Circuits (ICs)
- CPUs
- GPUs
- ASICs
- FPGAs
- Solar Cells

---

# Advantages

- High electrical conductivity.
- Large number of free electrons.
- Faster electron movement than holes.
- Essential for semiconductor devices.
- Improves device performance.

---

# Limitations

- Cannot be used alone to form most semiconductor devices.
- Requires accurate impurity concentration during doping.
- Must be combined with P-Type material to form a PN junction.

---

# Real-World Example

The **source** and **drain** regions of an **NMOS transistor** are heavily doped N-Type regions. The extra free electrons allow current to flow efficiently when the transistor is turned ON.

---

# Key Points

- N-Type semiconductor is an **extrinsic semiconductor**.
- Formed by **pentavalent doping**.
- Common impurities:
  - Phosphorus (P)
  - Arsenic (As)
  - Antimony (Sb)
- Majority charge carriers are **electrons**.
- Minority charge carriers are **holes**.
- Conductivity is higher than that of an intrinsic semiconductor.

# Summary

An **N-Type Semiconductor** is formed by adding pentavalent impurities such as phosphorus, arsenic, or antimony to a pure semiconductor. This doping process provides extra free electrons, making electrons the majority charge carriers and significantly increasing electrical conductivity. N-Type semiconductors are an essential part of diodes, transistors, MOSFETs, CMOS circuits, and integrated circuits.
